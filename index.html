01 / RESEARCH

Research that connects models and real-world systems.

How can we generate, execute, and analyze safety-critical scenarios systematically, and what reliability claims does the resulting evidence support?

RESEARCH PERSPECTIVE

“Autonomy is not just about making machines more capable. It is about making our confidence in them more deserved—through evidence and accountability.”

RESEARCH INTERESTS

My research interests span formal methods, autonomous systems and AI safety:

  • Formal verification of AI-enabled systems. I believe confidence in AI-enabled systems should be supported by explicit specifications and evidence. I investigate how formal verification and closed-loop simulation can be combined to assess their behaviour and safety.
  • Systematic and adaptive scenario generation. I develop methods that turn safety and coverage objectives into abstract traffic interaction scenarios, then concretize them into executable tests that challenge an AI-based ADS. I have developed adaptive testing methods that respond to the ADS during execution.
  • Runtime monitoring and failure analysis. I believe a failed test should help explain more than whether a requirement was violated. I use temporal logic and assume–guarantee contracts to relate observed behaviour to system requirements and component expectations.
  • Probabilistic verification and model refinement. I am interested in how uncertainty influences hazardous behaviour. I develop policy-guided tests using probabilistic model checking, and investigate how simulation feedback can refine Markov decision processes (MDPs), observation matching and action concretization.

METHODS IN MORE DETAIL

How the contributions work

Explore each method's main ideas, workflow and application to autonomous driving.

01VIVAS Coverage-directed symbolic scenario generation+

VIVAS combines a symbolic model of traffic interactions with coverage objectives expressed in Linear Temporal Logic (LTL). The nuXmv model checker generates abstract scenarios, which are then concretized into executable tests that challenge the AI-based ADS. The actual driving agent controls the ego in CARLA; the abstract model does not prescribe its concrete trajectory.

  1. Specify coverage. LTL objectives describe ordered traffic situations that a test should realize, without prescribing every intermediate state.
  2. Generate an abstract witness. The nuXmv model checker searches for a counterexample to the negated coverage objective, yielding a model-consistent trace that satisfies the desired objective.
  3. Concretize and execute. The witness determines surrounding-vehicle behavior and test parameters. CARLA executes the scenario with the ADS in the loop.
  4. Monitor the result. Execution traces are checked both for the intended scenario and for requirements such as collision avoidance.

A crash alone does not imply that the requested coverage objective was realized; conformance is checked separately.

02Contract-based failure analysis Monitoring and identification of component failures+

This method uses assume–guarantee contracts expressed in Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) to connect system-level requirements to expectations about constituent functions. A parent contract describes a higher-level requirement; child contracts capture the assumptions and guarantees of the components that refine it.

  1. Specify contracts. Express assumptions and guarantees in LTL for the system and its component hierarchy.
  2. Check refinements. Use OCRA and the nuXmv model checker at design time to verify the contract relationships.
  3. Monitor simulation traces. Interpret abstract predicates over concrete observations and inspect violated obligations during CARLA executions.
  4. Analyze and refine. Use observed violations to identify potential component-level causes and revise specifications or refinements when justified.

A monitor violation is evidence to investigate; its interpretation depends on the specification and the observability of its predicates.

03PGTG Probabilistic model checking and reactive test policies+

Policy-Guided Test Generation (PGTG) replaces a fixed abstract witness with a reactive test policy. The synthesized policy drives the testing environment towards behaviors most likely to realize the target property specification, adapting the selected actions to observations of the ego during simulation.

  1. Model choices and uncertainty. Build a Markov decision process (MDP) with non-deterministic choices for non-ego actions and probabilistic branches for the ego's perception and control outcomes.
  2. Specify a target. Express safety-critical states or component behaviors using Probabilistic Computation Tree Logic (PCTL) reachability properties.
  3. Synthesize a policy. The PRISM model checker resolves the non-deterministic choices into a policy that maximizes the target reachability probability in the MDP.
  4. Concretize and execute. Observe the simulation, match it to an abstract state and concretize the next policy action. Repeat this loop as the ADS responds, while monitoring the execution trace.

PGTG / ONGOING RESEARCH

Ego overtaking in CARLA

Current work extends policy-guided testing to a lane-changing ego interacting with two non-ego vehicles, including hazardous interactions involving a stationary vehicle.

RESEARCH QUESTION

How can iterative refinement of the MDP and concretization improve policy-guided exploration of hazardous states during ego overtaking?

Simulation feedback reveals mismatches between abstract transitions and concrete vehicle behavior. This evidence guides refinement of the MDP, observation matching and action concretization, followed by policy re-synthesis and further testing.

A related focus is hazard analysis along policy executions: how actions selected for one vehicle can lead the ego into danger involving another, including a static vehicle.
